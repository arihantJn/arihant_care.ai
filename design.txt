ajCares.ai — design and evaluation note

## What it is

ajCares.ai is a local appointment-scheduling demo. It uses fictional patients, policies, doctors, and appointment slots. It is not secure authentication or a production clinic system.

## Design choices

- **Python + FastAPI:** owns conversation state, rules, and scheduling tools.
- **React + Vite:** provides the patient-facing chat and booking interface.
- **SQLite + JSON:** store demo records and editable clinic, policy, and slot data.
- **Three bounded agents:** the scheduler can manage appointments; the clinic-information agent is read-only; the reminder agent creates opted-in local previews and sends no messages. Deterministic routing is enough for these roles. LangGraph is not used.
- **Narrow tools and explicit consent:** the scheduler checks availability, records a specific slot, and books, edits, or cancels. Booking requires a slot choice and separate confirmation. The agent reports success only after the tool confirms it.
- **RAG for clinic policies:** ChromaDB stores vectors created with MiniLM embeddings. Keyword/IDF retrieval remains a fallback. Similarity ranks results; it is not a confidence probability.
- **Optional local LLM:** Hugging Face Qwen2.5-0.5B can phrase answers from retrieved policy passages. It cannot choose slots or perform appointment actions. Deterministic cited answers remain the fallback.
- **Click-first UI:** patients choose visit type, date, specialty, doctor, and slot from options. This reduces typing and makes the next step clear. Free text is still available for policy questions.
- **Reviewed feedback:** ratings and reasons can inform future evaluation. They do not automatically change live behavior.

## Evaluation and improvement loop

The harness replays the same scenarios against isolated in-memory schedules. It checks the assistant's replies, tool events, and resulting appointments. Transcript checks alone cannot prove whether a booking happened.

| Run | Result | What happened |
|---|---:|---|
| Before reinforcement | **9/11 (82%)** | After several times were offered, an unqualified “yes” led to an unsupported booking claim. |
| After reinforcement | **11/11 (100%)** | The assistant asks for one specific slot, restates its date/time, and waits for confirmation. The happy-path booking still passes. |

The evaluator records the failure as a structured reinforcement, then reruns the same cases. This demonstrates a repeatable improvement loop on a small suite; it is not proof of real-world safety or autonomous model training.

## AI assistance and project-owner judgment

- AI helped scaffold code and documentation.
- The project owner directed the move to three specialist roles, local vector search, a small downloadable model, patient registration, feedback, and the ajCares.ai interface.
- Iterative feedback led to date/doctor/slot cards, appointment editing and cancellation, clearer date entry, and fewer required typed replies.
- Key design judgments: keep the LLM away from booking authority; limit each role's tools; require confirmation before changing appointments; use feedback for human-reviewed evaluation.

## Production priority

Replace name/date-of-birth lookup with verified authentication and authorization linked to the clinic's scheduling system. Add privacy and audit controls, clinician-approved policy content, and broader safety testing. Never enter real patient information in this demo.
